FOOT OF ANSAN, WEST OF HANSEONG, 1748 — Old Temple Finds a New Home on Ansan

An old temple in the western outskirts, traditionally said to have been founded by Master Doseon in the Silla period, has moved to a new site at the foot of Ansan. It formerly stood on fields near the Yeonhuigung palace grounds, in what is now the Sinchon area. Monks had long sought to leave the cramped and aging site. A new worship hall on the mountain's southern slope has finally been completed this year.

On hearing the news, the king personally bestowed the name Bongwonsa. The temple thus begins a new chapter with close royal ties. People in the capital already call it Saejeol, the 'new temple.' The open outlook toward the Han River and the mountain sheltering its rear have won praise for the new setting.

The relocation of a substantial temple so close to the capital is unusual in a Confucian state that keeps Buddhism at a distance. Some see a royal temple intended to pray for the household's well-being. Villagers crowded in to see the completed hall and bring offerings.

The monks say they will continue their Buddhist chants and ceremonies here. One elderly monk said that although the ground had changed, the light of the faith passed down for a thousand years would not go out. The new temple beneath the western mountain begins another chapter.
