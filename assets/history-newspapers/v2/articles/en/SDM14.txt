NORTHWEST OF HANSEONG, Late Joseon Period — Hongjewon Works Through the Night for Qing Envoys

Preparations are gathering pace at Hongjewon beyond Muakjae, northwest of the capital, as a Qing imperial embassy approaches. This state lodging is the envoys' final overnight stop before entering Hanyang. On arrival from Uiju they wash and change clothes, then form a ceremonial procession the following day to cross Muakjae and pass Mohwagwan and Yeongeunmun into the city.

Officials began repairing rooms and sweeping the courtyard days ago. Villagers have also been summoned to prepare food. Residents along the road have been ordered to prostrate themselves as the embassy passes. The costs of feeding and lodging the visitors, as well as their gifts, fall on the state and its people. One resident sighed that a single embassy could leave a village struggling for years.

A story tells of women returning from Qing captivity after the Manchu war washing in the stream outside Hongjewon before entering the capital. Its truth is uncertain. The area is nevertheless closely associated with painful national memories.

The formal obligations toward a powerful neighbor remain in force, but their weight rests on the people living along the route. At Hongjewon the lamps are still burning through the night.
