SEOUL, May 15, 1991 — Kang's Funeral Held; City Hall Memorial Blocked

A funeral for Kang Kyung-dae, the first-year Myongji University economics student beaten to death with steel pipes by police during a protest, was held yesterday morning on the university's campus in Namgajwa-dong, Seodaemun. It came 18 days after his death on April 26. Tens of thousands of students and citizens filled the grounds. Students lowered their heads before a portrait of the young man displayed beside the playing field.

The procession then left campus for a street memorial outside Seoul City Hall. Police blocked the routes into the city center, leading to a prolonged confrontation. The procession never reached City Hall, and the memorial was abandoned. Students remained around the hearse amid clouds of tear gas, while some citizens applauded from the roadside.

The funeral committee said it was furious that the government would not even let mourners send Kang off in peace and would arrange a new schedule. His death has prompted protests against the government and a succession of self-immolations across the country. With the dismissal of the interior minister followed by clashes over the funeral, political tensions show little sign of easing.

Students have begun calling the spot where Kang fell outside the main gate 'the martyr's place' and are calling for a memorial marker there.
