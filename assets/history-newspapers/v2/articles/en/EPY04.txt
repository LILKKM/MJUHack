NORTHWEST OF HANSEONG, 1459 — King Builds Temple for His Late Crown Prince

The king has founded a temple northwest of the capital to pray for his eldest son, Crown Prince Uigyeong, who died two years ago at the age of 20. Built near the prince's tomb, it will serve as a royal memorial temple and hold rites for his spirit.

Uigyeong was appointed crown prince after his father's accession. His learning and character had raised high expectations, but he fell ill and died suddenly. Dark rumors around the palace attributed the death to vengeful spirits connected with the fate of the former king Danjong. The king's grief at losing his son is said to have been profound.

Since taking the throne, the king has shown strong devotion to Buddhism. He has supported Korean-language translations of scriptures and the building and restoration of temples. The new foundation is widely seen as part of that effort. Royal funds paid for construction, and monks gathered for the first memorial rite when it was finished.

Some Confucian ministers disapprove of spending state resources on temples. Others say a parent cannot be blamed for mourning a child. The new temple beneath the northwestern hills will continue its prayers for the prince, carrying the royal household's sorrow.
