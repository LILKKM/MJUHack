HAN RIVER, WEST OF HANSEONG, May 1425 — Welcome Rain Gives Riverside Pavilion a Royal Name

With a prolonged drought leaving the country waiting for rain, the king traveled west of the capital to inspect the fields. On his return he stopped at Prince Hyoryeong's pavilion on a hill beside the Han River. Clouds gathered, and welcome rain began falling on the parched farmland.

Delighted, the king bestowed the name Huiujeong, meaning 'pavilion of welcome rain.' Hyoryeong, the king's second-eldest brother, built the pavilion last year on a hill overlooking the river. Its broad views of the water and the fields on the opposite bank have already drawn praise.

Farmers put down their tools and raised their hands toward the sky. One elderly farmer said rain arriving on the very day of the king's visit gave him hope for the harvest. At court, some attributed the shower to the ruler's concern for his people.

The pavilion is expected to host royal gatherings and receptions for foreign envoys. The drought has not fully broken, however, and the government continues to monitor reports from the provinces. The small riverside pavilion now carries a name recalling a king's concern for his people's hardship.
