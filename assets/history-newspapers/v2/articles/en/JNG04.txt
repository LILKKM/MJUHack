HANSEONG, October 30, 1898 — Officials and Citizens Adopt Six Reform Articles

A joint assembly of officials and citizens organized by the Independence Club has been meeting at the Jongno crossroads since the 28th. Ministers including Pak Jeong-yang, a senior official of the State Council, sat alongside ordinary people. The assembly adopted six articles setting out a program for national reform.

The articles call for officials and citizens to strengthen imperial authority together rather than depend on foreigners. Foreign concession agreements would require the joint signatures of ministers. State finances would be placed under the finance ministry, with budgets made public. Other demands include public trials for serious crimes, appointments to senior office based on collective opinion and adherence to established law.

Aristocrats and officials have been joined on the platform by merchants, students, women and even members of the butcher caste. A speaker from that community reportedly won loud applause by saying the country and its people could prosper only through cooperation between government and citizens.

The voices filling the crossroads before Bosingak have made a deep impression on the capital. Rumors persist, however, that some ministers are warning the emperor that the Independence Club intends to overturn the state. Whether the resolutions will become policy or meet resistance remains uncertain.
