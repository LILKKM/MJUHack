HANSEONG, Autumn 1840 — Scholar Kim Jeong-hui Sent into Exile on Jeju

Kim Jeong-hui, the celebrated calligrapher and scholar and former vice minister of war, is to be exiled to Daejeong on Jeju Island. He was arrested and severely interrogated after a case involving Yun Sang-do from ten years earlier was reopened. His life was reportedly in danger before senior minister Jo In-yeong and others intervened, securing a reduction of the punishment from death to exile.

Daejeong is among the remotest parts of Jeju. Kim is sentenced to confinement within a thorn enclosure surrounding his residence. Writers and scholars have expressed sorrow for a man who, at 55, must now face a difficult sea crossing.

As a young man, Kim visited Beijing and studied epigraphy and textual scholarship with the Qing scholars Weng Fanggang and Ruan Yuan. After returning, he astonished scholars by identifying the monument on Bukhansan's Bibong as a stele recording a royal tour by Silla's King Jinheung. His calligraphy has been praised for mastering traditional forms while opening a new expressive path.

Many see court factionalism as the force driving a great scholar overseas. Devoted students are offering to send books and brushes to his place of exile. People bowed their heads as he left his old home in the capital.
