GYEONGSEONG, October 1920 — Aunae Independence Protest Leader Dies in Prison

Yu Gwan-sun, 18, arrested after leading an independence demonstration last March at Aunae Market in Byeongcheon, Cheonan, has died in Seodaemun Prison. Her death occurred on the 28th of last month. Ewha School representatives reportedly recovered her body and buried her at Itaewon Cemetery on the 14th of this month.

An Ewha student, Yu joined the demonstrations in Gyeongseong last March. When her school closed, she returned home and helped prepare a market rally. On April 1 she distributed Korean flags and led cries for independence before thousands of people. Both her parents were killed when Japanese military police opened fire that day.

Following her arrest and trial in Gongju, a three-year prison sentence was upheld by the Gyeongseong appellate court, and she was confined at Seodaemun. Fellow prisoners say she remained defiant. On March 1 this year, she again called for independence inside the prison to mark the movement's first anniversary. Harsh treatment afterward reportedly left her gravely injured.

The authorities have not disclosed a detailed cause of death. Her schoolmates could scarcely bring themselves to weep aloud for the young woman. Many others arrested for the independence movement remain behind the red-brick walls outside Seodaemun.
