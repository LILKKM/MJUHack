GAHOE-DONG, GYEONGSEONG, March 1, 1919 — Independence Leaders Change Declaration Venue

Leaders of Cheondogyo, Christianity and Buddhism gathered last night at Cheondogyo leader Son Byeong-hui's Gahoe-dong home for a final meeting before today's independence declaration. They are among the national representatives who signed the document. More than 20 representatives are understood to have attended.

The original plan was to read the declaration with students and citizens at Tapgol Park. The meeting instead chose Taehwagwan, a restaurant in Insadong, out of concern that a large, excited crowd might clash with Japanese police and suffer heavy casualties. The representatives reportedly decided to inform police themselves after the declaration and accept arrest.

Gahoe-dong and neighboring Gye-dong have been central to preparations. Teachers and young leaders met early on in the night-duty room at Jungang School. At Boseongsa, the Cheondogyo printing office, more than 20,000 copies of the declaration were secretly printed and distributed around the country.

After noon today, cries for Korean independence are expected to ring out in the heart of the city. Attention now turns to whether the resolve formed in a Bukchon hanok meeting room will swell into a movement across the nation.
