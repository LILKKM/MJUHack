HANSEONG, Winter 1519 — Jo Gwang-jo and Reformist Scholars Swept Up in Purge

An overnight upheaval at court has brought the arrest and imprisonment of Chief Inspector Jo Gwang-jo and other young scholars. Meritorious officials including Hong Gyeong-ju, Nam Gon and Sim Jeong reportedly entered the palace secretly at night to accuse Jo's circle of forming a faction and disturbing the state.

Enjoying the king's strong confidence, Jo had led reform for several years. He abolished the office for Daoist rites and introduced a recommendation-based examination to select officials. More recently he pressed for unearned honors to be stripped from some men rewarded for the earlier coup, provoking intense opposition among its beneficiaries.

Rumors even claim that honey was used to write characters on leaves so insects would eat out a message suggesting that a Jo should become king. Hundreds of Sungkyunkwan scholars gathered before the palace gate to proclaim Jo's innocence but failed to change the king's mind.

Jo is to be exiled to Neungju in Jeolla Province. Court circles are already filled with talk that he may receive a sentence of death by poison. Public opinion is divided between those who believe the idealistic scholars moved too quickly and those who say corrupt officials have driven out loyal servants.
