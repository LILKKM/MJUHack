SEOUL, December 2014 — Eunpyeong Museum Opens a Window on Hanok and History

The Eunpyeong History Hanok Museum has opened at the entrance to Eunpyeong Hanok Village in Jingwan-dong. It brings together objects excavated during the Eunpyeong New Town development, accounts of earlier local life and explanations of traditional Korean house construction.

During Joseon, the area lay on the main road from the capital to Uiju. Royal tombs, temples and shrines stood beneath Bukhansan. The new town works uncovered thousands of Joseon-period graves and many everyday objects, drawing scholarly attention. These findings form the basis of the museum's account of Eunpyeong's past.

Displays include excavated pottery, ornaments and burial goods. Models and hands-on areas explain how hanok are built. A large reproduction of the Daedongyeojido map covers the entrance floor, allowing visitors to walk across it and locate old Seoul and Eunpyeong.

Museum officials say they hope residents will recognize centuries of history beneath neighborhoods transformed into apartment districts. They also plan historical exploration programs linked with Jingwansa, Geumseongdang and the Bukhansan walking trail. The new town now has a place for its older memories.
