HANSEONG, Spring 1597 — Admiral Yi Sun-sin Imprisoned in the Capital

Yi Sun-sin, commander of the naval forces of the three provinces, has been arrested, brought to Hanyang and confined at the State Tribunal. The court accuses him of failing to send his fleet despite orders as Japanese forces returned across the sea, allowing the enemy to escape. He is also accused of deceiving the king. The dispute concerns his refusal to sail after intelligence claimed the Japanese commander Kato Kiyomasa was crossing to Korea.

Naval circles counter that the intelligence may itself have been an enemy trap and that a reckless sortie could have cost the fleet. Some say court factionalism has turned a commander responsible for victories at Okpo and Hansando since the first invasion into a criminal overnight.

Yi is reportedly undergoing harsh interrogation. Execution was at one point discussed, but senior official Jeong Tak petitioned that a commander of such achievement should not be killed and pleaded for his life.

Won Gyun has taken command of the southern navy. People increasingly ask who will defend the sea without Admiral Yi. Attention in the capital is fixed on the tribunal and whether he will ever return to his fleet.
