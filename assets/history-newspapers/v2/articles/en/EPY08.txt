SEOUL, May 2009 — Hidden Independence Flag Found at Jingwansa

A colonial-period Korean flag and a bundle of independence newspapers have been found inside the altar area during dismantling and repair work at Jingwansa's Chilseonggak in Eunpyeong. Wrapped in old cloth, the flag was made by adding the taegeuk symbol and four trigrams over a Japanese flag. It has holes resembling burn damage. The accompanying newspapers, dated to around 1919, include The Independent, Joseon Dongnip Sinmun and Jayu Sinjongbo.

The temple and scholars believe the objects were hidden around the March First Movement by Baek Cho-wol, then a monk here. He led a secret Buddhist independence organization, raised funds and supported the provisional government. He was repeatedly arrested and imprisoned and is understood to have died in custody before liberation.

The flag, which places Korean symbols over a Japanese emblem, is being interpreted as an act of resistance. Specialists call it vivid evidence of Buddhist participation in the independence movement. The discovery of a colonial-period Korean flag in a temple is highly unusual.

Jingwansa plans to conserve the objects before displaying them publicly. After 90 years concealed inside the altar, the flag has emerged to recall the independence movement's voices.
