HANSEONG, November 1896 — Independence Gate Foundation Stone Laid

A foundation-laying ceremony for Independence Gate was held on the 21st outside Seodaemun, where Yeongeunmun once stood. The demolished gate had welcomed Qing envoys and had come to symbolize Joseon's subordination to China. A gate bearing the word 'independence' is now to rise in its place.

The project was first proposed by Seo Jae-pil after his return from the United States. The Independence Club, in which he plays a leading role, is overseeing construction. Since opening in July, the club has appealed for donations through the press. Members of the royal household, officials, merchants, students and day laborers have all contributed. Donors' names are being published in The Independent.

The planned granite arch takes its inspiration from the Arc de Triomphe in Paris. A Korean flag is to be carved at the top, with the gate's name inscribed in Korean and Chinese characters on the front and back. The two stone supports remaining from Yeongeunmun are to be retained as reminders of the past.

Officials, foreign legation representatives, students and citizens gathered to sing and watch the foundation stone being laid. One elderly man grew tearful, saying he had never expected to witness such a day on the road where people once prostrated themselves before imperial envoys. Completion is planned for next year.
