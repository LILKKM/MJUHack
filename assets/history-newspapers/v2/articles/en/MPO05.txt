SEOUL, February 11, 1968 — Bamseom Blasted Apart for Yeouido Development

Bamseom, the island in the middle of Seoul's Han River, was blasted apart with dynamite yesterday afternoon. The city said the demolition would supply rock and earth for the embankment around Yeouido and widen the river channel to improve flood drainage.

Named for its chestnut-like shape, Bamseom had housed boatbuilders and fishing families for centuries. Before the blast, roughly 60 resident households were relocated together to the foot of Wausan in Changjeon-dong, Mapo. People born and raised on the island watched from the opposite bank with tears in their eyes.

One resident said ancestral land had disappeared in a single day, while the steep replacement plots offered little prospect of a livelihood. Islanders had built boats and grown peanuts and vegetables. Those ways of supporting themselves are now gone.

Seoul has announced plans to make Yeouido a new urban center. Officials say the encircling embankment will create extensive building land on what were sand flats. Critics argue that measures for the displaced residents remain inadequate. Development is redrawing the map of the Han River.
