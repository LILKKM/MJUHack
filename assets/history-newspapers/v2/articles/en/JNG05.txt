JONGNO, SEOUL, Present Day — Seoul's Quiet Center Hides in a Building Lobby

Where is Seoul's geographical center? The answer lies, unexpectedly, inside a building lobby in Insadong, Jongno. A small stone marker on the ground floor of the Hanaro Building on Insadong 5-gil identifies the spot. Passersby often fail to notice its significance.

The center was calculated from the city's administrative boundaries and area. Joseon's capital, Hanyang, was planned around Gyeongbokgung, Jongmyo, the Sajik altars and Unjongga, its main commercial street. Remembering that this old center lay around Jongno and Insadong makes it striking that Seoul's midpoint remains near the historic capital centuries later.

No particular major event occurred at the marker itself. A short walk, however, leads to the former Taehwagwan site, where the March First declaration was read, Tapgol Park, the Six Licensed Shops site and Unhyeongung. Quiet in itself, the marker offers a starting point for exploring some of Seoul's great historical stages.

A building representative said visitors occasionally enter specifically to find the stone. Because it stands indoors, access depends on the building's opening hours. Seoul's 'navel' is a place one must know to seek out.
