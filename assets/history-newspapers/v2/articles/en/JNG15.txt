SEOUL, December 2012 — Former Government Offices Reopen as History Museum

The former Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism building beside Gwanghwamun Square has reopened as the National Museum of Korean Contemporary History. It is the first national museum devoted to the country's modern and contemporary history, tracing its course from the opening of Korea's ports to the present.

The building is one of a pair constructed side by side in 1961 with American assistance. It housed the Economic Planning Board, which devised and directed the five-year development plans. Government offices associated with planning the Miracle on the Han River have thus become a place to look back on that history. The twin building next door still serves as the United States Embassy.

Exhibits follow a chronological sequence through independence activism, liberation, the establishment of the government, the Korean War, industrialization and democratization. Everyday objects from the industrialization years appear alongside material on the democracy movement. The rooftop garden overlooks Gyeongbokgung and Gwanghwamun Square.

Some exhibits drew criticism before the opening for allegedly glorifying or minimizing particular periods. Debate over historical interpretation is expected to continue. Museum officials say they will hear a range of views and improve the displays. Gwanghwamun now has another place to ask how recent history should be remembered.
