SEOUL, April 29, 1993 — Cheon Sang-byeong, Poet of 'Return to Heaven,' Dies

Cheon Sang-byeong, the poet widely loved for 'Return to Heaven,' which likens life to a brief outing, died yesterday at the age of 63. His long-standing liver cirrhosis is reported to have worsened.

Cheon emerged in literary circles in the 1950s, attracting attention for his clear, innocent poetic voice. In 1967 he was implicated in the so-called East Berlin affair and subjected to severe torture, leaving lasting physical and psychological damage. In 1971 he collapsed in the street and disappeared. Believing him dead, friends published The Bird as a posthumous collection. His subsequent return from a psychiatric hospital gave rise to the story of a poet who lived to see his own posthumous book.

In 1972 he married Mok Sun-ok, the sister of a friend. He continued writing beside his wife, who ran the Gwicheon teahouse in Insadong. Poor but rarely without a smile, he was called the last poet of innocence. Many remember his happiness at a cup of makgeolli or a single thousand-won note.

Fellow writers paid tribute to a man whose clear gaze, they said, had survived torture and poverty. As in the poem that imagined life as an outing, he has now finished that journey and returned to heaven. Writers and readers who loved his work continue to visit the mourning hall.
