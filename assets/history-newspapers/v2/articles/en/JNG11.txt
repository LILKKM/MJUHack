SEOUL, June 27, 1949 — Kim Gu Assassinated at Gyeonggyojang

Kim Gu, the former leader of the Korean Provisional Government known by his pen name Baekbeom, was shot dead yesterday in his second-floor office at Gyeonggyojang in Seodaemun. He was 74. The assailant has been identified as Ahn Doo-hee, an active-duty army artillery second lieutenant. Ahn reportedly drew a pistol and fired several shots during a meeting with Kim.

Secretaries rushed in at the sound of gunfire and seized the gunman, but Kim was already dead. As news spread, large crowds quickly assembled outside. Mourners cried and struck the ground in grief, expressing anguish that such a killing could occur just four years after liberation.

Since returning from China in November 1945, Kim had lived at Gyeonggyojang, defended the provisional government's legitimacy and led opposition to trusteeship. Last year he crossed the 38th parallel for a joint North-South meeting in Pyongyang in an effort to prevent partition. He opposed a separate southern government to the end.

The military says it is investigating the motive and possible forces behind the killing. Public suspicions about those responsible are multiplying. The government has decided on a national funeral. A veteran campaigner for a united country has died without seeing that dream fulfilled.
