GYEONGSEONG, March 1, 1919 — Thousands March for Independence from Tapgol Park

Thousands of students and citizens assembled at Tapgol Park in Jongno this afternoon, read the declaration of independence and marched into the streets calling for Korean independence. Students had gathered from around noon. When the national representatives did not appear, they decided to read the document themselves. A young man mounted the octagonal pavilion and read as the crowd cheered and threw hats into the air.

The marchers left the park, passed the Jongno crossroads and divided into several groups. Some headed for Daehanmun at Deoksugung, where Gojong's mourning altar stands; others moved toward Namdaemun, Seoul Station and the French and American consulates. Carrying Korean flags, they were joined by merchants, workers and women along the roads, and the procession rapidly grew.

At Taehwagwan in Insadong, Son Byeong-hui and other national representatives separately declared independence and were taken into police custody. They reportedly changed venues out of fear that a crowd confrontation would cause casualties.

Japanese police and military police began dispersing demonstrators toward evening and arrested many. Reports say demonstrations also broke out today in Pyongyang, Uiju, Wonsan and elsewhere. Provincial visitors gathered for Gojong's funeral are expected to carry the movement's message across the country.
