SEOUL, December 1995 — Jongmyo Added to UNESCO World Heritage List

Jongmyo, the Joseon royal ancestral shrine in Seoul, has been inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List. The World Heritage Committee, meeting in Berlin, Germany, also approved Seokguram Grotto and Bulguksa Temple in Gyeongju and the Tripitaka storage halls at Haeinsa in Hapcheon. These are Korea's first cultural sites to receive the designation.

King Taejo established Jongmyo alongside Gyeongbokgung in 1395 when he made Hanyang the capital. It has housed the spirit tablets of successive kings and queens and served their ancestral rites. Burned during the Japanese invasions, it was rebuilt under Gwanghaegun. The main hall was extended sideways as the number of tablets increased, creating an exceptionally long single wooden building.

The committee praised its preservation of the architectural form of a Confucian ritual space. It particularly valued the continuation of the rites over centuries. At the annual royal ancestral ceremony, descendants follow traditional procedures accompanied by Jongmyo ritual music.

Cultural circles have welcomed international recognition of the heritage's value. Concerns remain that surrounding urban development could damage its setting. The listing has prompted calls for more careful conservation measures.
