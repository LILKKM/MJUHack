HANSEONG, 1894 — Gabo Reforms Close Royal Grain Storehouse

Gwangheungchang, the Sogang storehouse that has supplied officials' salaries since Joseon's founding, is to close. The Gabo reforms under way since this summer are transforming public administration. With taxes shifting from rice and cloth to money, and officials also to be paid in cash, the salary-grain warehouse has lost its purpose.

The great state storehouse stands near the Sogang landing on the Han River. Each year boats carrying tax grain from Jeolla, Chungcheong and other provinces rounded the western coast and traveled upriver to unload here. Porters kept the landing busy, while servants from officials' households lined up with carts on salary days.

Recently, however, the warehouses have often stood empty. Complaints have mounted over embezzled grain, waterlogged rice and rice mixed with sand. The soldiers' uprising 12 years ago, provoked when overdue pay was issued in rice adulterated with chaff and sand, also had its roots in the grain system.

An elderly Sogang boatman said he had spent his life carrying tax grain and no longer knew what cargo would replace it. Others hope the new arrangements will reduce corruption. A centuries-old age of grain storehouses is drawing to a close.
