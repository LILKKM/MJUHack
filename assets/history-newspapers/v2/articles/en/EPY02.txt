HANSEONG, Autumn 1711 — Bukhansan Fortress Completed in Six Months

A major mountain fortress on Bukhansan, north of the capital, has been completed in half a year. Work began this spring, with three military commands dividing responsibility for the sections and laying stone day and night. The walls follow the mountain's rugged peaks and ridges. A royal refuge, storehouses and temples inside are intended to let the king and people withstand a prolonged emergency.

The court pressed ahead with construction in response to the lessons of the Manchu invasion decades ago. To avoid a repetition of the king's retreat to Namhansan Fortress and eventual surrender, officials argued that a strong defensive base was needed close to the capital. Monks are to be stationed at temples throughout the fortress to help guard it.

Opposition has also been substantial. Some ministers warned that the expense and labor demands would burden the people and petitioned that defending the capital should come first. Soldiers and civilians called up for the work endured great hardship carrying stones over steep mountain paths.

The king reportedly plans to inspect the fortress next spring. The long walls across Bukhansan's granite ridges offer residents both reassurance and a visible expression of the wish never to suffer another invasion.
