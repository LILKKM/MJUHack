SEODAEMUN, SEOUL, 1970s — Moraenae Market Keeps a Stream's Name Alive

The alleys of Moraenae Market in Namgajwa-dong, Seodaemun, were busy with shoppers from early morning. Stalls selling fish, vegetables and clothing line both sides of the narrow lanes. Merchants speak proudly of a market once counted among Seoul's four largest.

The name Moraenae comes from an older name for Hongjecheon, the stream running beside the market. Water descending from Bukhansan and Inwangsan is said to have carried enough sand to keep the streambed white. Its Chinese-character name was Sacheon, meaning sandy stream. The name survives in Sacheongyo, the bridge that crosses it.

Local accounts also differ over the origin of Gajwa. One holds that the area was called Gajaeul because crayfish were plentiful. Another interprets the name as referring to the edge of a village. Older residents recall catching crayfish and swimming in the stream as children.

No major battle or single dramatic event defines this place. Instead, sand carried by a stream became a place-name, which in turn named a market and a bridge. Generations of neighborhood memory remain in those names. Development is changing the waterway, but residents hope Moraenae will endure.
