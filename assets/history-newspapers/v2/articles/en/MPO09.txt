HANSEONG, Autumn 1866 — Catholic Executions Mount at Yanghwajin Bluff

Since foreign vessels sailed up the Han River to Yanghwajin and withdrew, the court has intensified its persecution of Catholics. Believers are being brought to Jamdubong, the hill above the landing, and beheaded. Named for its resemblance to a silkworm raising its head, the scenic rocky bluff once attracted scholars composing poetry.

The Daewongun is reported to have called for land defiled by Western ships to be cleansed with the blood of Catholics. Following the execution of French priests and many believers this spring, the very place visited by the ships has become an execution ground. Those brought here include aristocrats, commoners, women and young people. The exact number killed is unknown.

Boatmen using the landing turn away from the sounds on the hill. The condemned reportedly continue praying even at the execution ground. One onlooker said their steadiness in the face of death was itself unsettling.

The court says it intends to root out people at home who might cooperate with Western powers. No one knows when the bloodshed will end. People have already begun calling the bluff the 'mountain of severed heads.'
