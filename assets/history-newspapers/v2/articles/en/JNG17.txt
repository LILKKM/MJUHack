SEOUL, May 2002 — Seoul's History Finds a Home on Old Palace Grounds

The Seoul Museum of History has opened on the former Gyeonghuigung palace grounds in Sinmunno, Jongno. Dedicated to the city's history, it traces six centuries from Joseon's capital Hanyang to the metropolis of today.

The site itself reflects the city's upheavals. Gyeonghuigung was built under Gwanghaegun and later used by Kings Yeongjo and Jeongjo. Most of its halls were dismantled during the reconstruction of Gyeongbokgung. Under Japanese rule, a school for Japanese students occupied the grounds. Its main gate, Heunghwamun, and principal hall, Sungjeongjeon, were sold and moved elsewhere before portions of the palace were eventually restored.

Galleries display maps, paintings and everyday objects from Joseon-era Hanyang. Photographs and documents show changes through the opening of the ports, colonial rule and the years after liberation. Visitors gather happily around a detailed city model to find their own neighborhoods.

Museum officials say many Seoul residents know little of the city's past and hope to create a space for sharing its memory. The work of recovering a city's history has begun on the site of a largely vanished palace.
