DANGINRI, GYEONGSEONG, 1930 — Danginri's First Coal-Fired Unit Begins Operation

Korea's first full-scale thermal power station has opened at Danginri on the Han River west of Gyeongseong. Built by the Gyeongseong Electric Company, its first generating unit has been completed and has begun producing electricity. Smoke from the enormous chimney is visible as far away as Yeouido and Noryangjin.

The city has relied largely on hydroelectric power and small generating facilities. During dry periods, voltage has dropped and lamps have flickered. The operator says the new coal-fired equipment will provide a more reliable supply. Coal arrives by boat and rail and is piled high beside the station.

Demand for electricity is rising each year as trams, factories and illuminated shops multiply. Access, however, remains concentrated in the largely Japanese southern districts, government offices and big factories. Many Korean neighborhood lanes still depend on oil lamps. High electricity charges are another source of complaint.

Nearby residents worry about machinery running day and night and the spread of coal dust. One said the riverside village seemed to be turning into a factory town. The operator plans further expansion.
