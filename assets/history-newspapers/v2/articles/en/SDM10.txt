WESTERN OUTSKIRTS OF HANSEONG, 1598 — Stele Honors Yang Hao as Ming Commander Returns Home

Yang Hao, the Ming military commissioner who came to Joseon with Chinese troops during the renewed Japanese invasion, has been recalled home. A commemorative stele has been raised beside the road west of the capital to honor him. Last autumn Japanese forces swept through Namwon and Jeonju and threatened the capital. Yang pressed his troops forward and checked the Japanese vanguard at Jiksan, a battle widely credited with halting their northward advance.

In winter, Yang besieged the Japanese commander Kato Kiyomasa at Ulsan Castle. Cold weather and enemy reinforcements prevented its capture. Accounts circulating around the court say disputes over his achievements in the Ming government led to the recall.

The Joseon court has expressed regret at his departure, saying it cannot forget his role in defending the capital. Residents lined the road to see the returning procession off. A shrine being established alongside the commemoration, Seonmusa, is to honor the Ming commander Xing Jie.

The war is not over. Japanese forces still occupy the southern coast, and the people remain impoverished. Some voice unease at the need to carve a foreign general's achievements in stone. What this roadside monument comes to represent will depend on how the war ends.
