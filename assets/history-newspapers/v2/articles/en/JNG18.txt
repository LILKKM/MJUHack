BELOW INWANGSAN, HANSEONG, Intercalary Fifth Month, 1751 — Jeong Seon Captures Inwangsan After the Rain

Word is spreading through artistic circles that the elderly painter Jeong Seon, known as Gyeomjae, has depicted Inwangsan after the monsoon rain on a large sheet. He lives in Jang-dong beneath the mountain west of the capital. Well into his seventies, he is said to retain complete command of his brush.

Repeated layers of dark ink give the mountain's immense rock peaks the weight of rain-soaked granite. Mist rising after the shower wraps a white band around the slopes. Admirers say this work brings his approach to a peak: painting the Korean landscape before his eyes rather than imagining Chinese scenery in the older manner.

Jeong has spent his life traveling to paint Geumgangsan, the Han River and places inside and outside the capital. Jang-dong beneath Inwangsan and Baegaksan, where he was born and raised, has repeatedly appeared as a home landscape in his work.

Some artists suggest he painted the scene while worrying about the illness of his longtime friend, the poet Yi Byeong-yeon. If so, the mountain clearing after rain may express a hope that his friend will recover with the strength of its granite. Jang-dong's lanes have once more become the setting for an admired work.
