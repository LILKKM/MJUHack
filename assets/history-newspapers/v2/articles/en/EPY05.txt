SEOUL, 2001 — Royal Consort's Grave Marker Returns After Four Centuries

A grave marker for Lady Sim, a royal consort of Joseon's King Seongjong, has returned to Korea after more than 400 years abroad. Believed to have been taken to Japan during the Japanese invasions, the stone was discovered in Tokyo last year. Negotiations over its return brought it home this year.

Lady Sim, who held the rank Sugyong, was the mother of Princesses Gyeongsun and Sukhye and Princes Iseong and Yeongsan. She died in 1515. Such markers stood before graves and recorded the status and lives of those buried. The returned stone describes her life and children and is valued as evidence of early Joseon funeral practices and royal etiquette.

Exactly when and how the marker reached Japan remains unclear. Scholars consider removal during the invasions likely, given accounts of Japanese troops disturbing royal and other tombs and taking large quantities of stonework and cultural objects.

Experts say many Korean cultural objects in Japan have yet to return and call for systematic research and recovery efforts. Discussions are under way on preserving the marker in Jingwan-dong, Eunpyeong, near the original grave site. A small stone's return from a long absence is bringing forgotten history back into view.
