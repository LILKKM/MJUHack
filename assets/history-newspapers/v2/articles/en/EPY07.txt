NORTHWEST OF HANSEONG, 1397 — Jingwansa Chosen for State Rites for the Dead

The king has ordered a Suryuksa ritual establishment at Jingwansa beneath Samgaksan, northwest of the capital. State-sponsored Suryuk rites are to be held there. The Buddhist ceremony offers food to wandering spirits of the water and land and seeks to guide them to paradise.

Wars and upheavals during the founding of the new dynasty have cost many lives. The king appears to regard comforting the dead as part of securing the kingdom's stability. Some say the rites may also be intended for the spirits of Goryeo's royal family. The temple was reportedly chosen for its proximity to the capital and its deep, quiet mountain setting.

Jingwansa is traditionally understood to have been founded in honor of the monk Jingwan, who sheltered Goryeo's future King Hyeonjong when his life was in danger. The new designation gives it close ties to the Joseon royal house as well.

Some ministers seeking to establish Confucianism as the state's foundation object to government sponsorship of Buddhist ceremonies. Most, however, accept that consoling the dead cannot simply be forbidden. The mountain temple's bells are now to accompany rites addressing the new kingdom's grief.
