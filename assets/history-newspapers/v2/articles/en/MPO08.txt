SEOUL, July 10, 1987 — Vast Crowds Join Lee Han-yeol's Funeral

A public funeral for democracy was held yesterday for Lee Han-yeol, the Yonsei business student who died on July 5, 26 days after being struck by a tear-gas projectile. After a campus service in front of the main building, the procession passed through Sinchon and Ahyeon-dong to Seoul City Hall. Police estimated hundreds of thousands attended, while organizers put the crowd at more than a million.

Lee was struck in the head by a police projectile outside the university gate on June 9, the day before the June 10 national rally. His injury helped ignite the June democracy movement. Students, citizens and office workers took to the streets across the country. On June 29, the ruling party leader announced acceptance of a constitutional change permitting direct presidential elections.

At the street memorial outside City Hall, mourners called for Lee to be brought back to life. His mother's sobbing before his portrait left the crowd in grief. The procession later set out for his hometown, Gwangju, where he is to be buried at Mangwol-dong cemetery.

There were clashes between marchers and police in some areas, but the funeral was largely peaceful. The country is watching where the democratic path opened by this young man's death will lead.
