BUKCHON, HANSEONG, Spring 1795 — Secret Mass Held in Bukchon Home

Zhou Wenmo, the Qing Chinese priest who secretly crossed the border into Hanyang last winter, reportedly celebrated Easter Mass in a private home in Bukchon. It is the first Mass celebrated by an ordained priest in this country. The service took place at the home of Choe In-gil, a Catholic and former interpreter. Believers are said to have sheltered the priest there and assembled secretly at night.

Catholicism began spreading here through lay believers more than a decade ago, after the scholar Yi Seung-hun returned from baptism in Qing China. Without a priest, believers conducted services themselves before learning that the practice did not comply with Church rules. They repeatedly asked the bishop in Beijing to send a priest. After Yun Yu-il and others risked their lives traveling to Beijing, Zhou crossed the Yalu River.

Those attending reportedly wept as they received Communion for the first time. Their joy was short-lived: rumors now suggest that the priest's presence has been reported and that the police have begun a search.

The court regards Catholicism, which rejects ancestral rites, as a dangerous teaching that threatens the state's foundations. The punishment awaiting those who hid the priest is uncertain. Believers fear what may follow the service in this small tiled-roof house in Bukchon.
