HANSEONG, Intercalary Fifth Month, 1762 — Crown Prince Dies After Eight Days in Rice Chest

Crown Prince Sado, confined on King Yeongjo's orders in a rice chest before Munjeongjeon at Changgyeonggung, has reportedly died after eight days. He was 28. The death of the heir at his own father's hands has shocked the palace and the country.

Accounts circulating around the court say the king severely rebuked the prince's conduct and ordered him stripped of his title. He then had a rice chest brought and commanded the prince to enter it. The prince reportedly pleaded tearfully for forgiveness, but the king would not relent. The young royal grandson is also said to have prostrated himself before his grandfather to beg for his father's life.

The prince had been considered gifted from childhood. For years, however, reports had circulated that estrangement from his stern father had contributed to mental illness. Rumors that he had harmed people in the palace further inflamed the king's anger. Some observers also see court factional conflict behind the tragedy.

After the death, the king mourned and granted the posthumous name Sado, meaning to remember with sorrow. Concern is growing for the future of the 11-year-old royal grandson. The rice chest in Munjeongjeon's courtyard has become a lasting wound in the royal household's history.
