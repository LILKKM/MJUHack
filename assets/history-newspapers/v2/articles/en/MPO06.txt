HANSEONG, April 1894 — Kim Ok-gyun's Body Dismembered at Yanghwajin

The body of Kim Ok-gyun, who spent a decade in exile in Japan after leading the Gapsin Coup, has been subjected to posthumous dismemberment at the Yanghwajin landing. Kim was shot dead last month at a Shanghai inn by Hong Jong-u, an assassin sent by the Joseon government. His remains were returned aboard a Qing warship, then taken to Yanghwajin west of the capital for the punishment.

His severed head was displayed on a pole with a notice branding him a traitor. Crowds gathered at the landing. The court said it had dealt firmly with treason. Japanese and Western legations, however, reportedly condemned the mutilation of a man already dead as barbaric.

Kim studied reformist ideas under Pak Gyu-su. Ten years ago he attempted to establish a new government through a coup launched during the opening banquet of the postal office. Qing military intervention ended the effort after three days, and he escaped to Japan. Most of his associates and family members lost their lives.

A person at the landing said no one had imagined that a man who once sought to change the country would return like this. Tensions between Qing China and Japan over his death are mounting, raising fears of an intensifying struggle over Joseon.
