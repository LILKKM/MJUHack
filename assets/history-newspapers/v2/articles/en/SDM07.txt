HANSEONG, February 1624 — Royal Forces Rout Yi Gwal's Rebels at Ansan

Yi Gwal's rebel army has suffered a major defeat on Ansan, west of the capital. Yi raised his troops in Yeongbyeon, Pyeongan Province, after growing resentful of his treatment despite his part in last year's coup. When his son was also accused of treason, he led elite troops south. Defeating government forces on the way, the rebels drove the king to flee to Gongju, occupied the capital and even installed another ruler.

Pursuing troops under commander Jang Man seized the summit of Ansan, at Gilmajae, overnight on the advice of Jeong Chung-sin and Nam Yi-heung. Underestimating the troops on the heights, the rebels attacked the next day. Wind blew dust into their eyes. Striking downhill, government forces broke the rebel ranks and sent them fleeing.

Residents of the capital are said to have watched the smoke and heard the shouts from the western ridge in fear. The Ansan beacon site, normally used to relay urgent news by fire and smoke, had itself become a battlefield.

Yi fled east with the survivors. Reports soon followed that his own men had killed him. The court is preparing for the king's return. The uprising, just a year after the change of regime, has exposed deep distrust within the government.
