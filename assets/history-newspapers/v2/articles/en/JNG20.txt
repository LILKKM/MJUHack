INSADONG, GYEONGSEONG, March 1, 1919 — Leaders Declare Independence, Then Accept Arrest

Twenty-nine national representatives, including Son Byeong-hui, gathered at 2 p.m. today in a private room at Taehwagwan restaurant in Insadong and declared Korean independence. Thirty-three Cheondogyo, Christian and Buddhist leaders signed the declaration; those at the meeting were the representatives able to attend, apart from several outside the city.

After reading the declaration in turn and hearing brief remarks from Han Yong-un, the representatives called three times for Korean independence. The document proclaims Korea an independent state and its people free. While condemning Japan's wrongdoing, it calls for a new era founded on justice and humanity rather than resentment.

The representatives themselves then notified the Japanese police. Officers and military police soon arrived and took them all into custody. They reportedly offered no resistance as they entered the vehicles.

At the same time, thousands of students and citizens at nearby Tapgol Park read the declaration separately and poured into the streets. Demonstrators spread toward Jongno, Namdaemun and Daehanmun. Visitors from the provinces attending Emperor Gojong's funeral helped swell the crowds. Observers expect the movement to spread nationwide.
