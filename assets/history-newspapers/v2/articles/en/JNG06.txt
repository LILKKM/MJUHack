ANGUK, HANSEONG, Spring 1882 — Crown Prince Weds at New Andong Palace

The crown prince, the king's eldest son, has celebrated his wedding at the newly built Andong detached palace. The daughter of Min Tae-ho of the Yeoheung Min family was selected as crown princess. She stayed at the palace beforehand to learn court etiquette. The prince then came in person for the ceremony of receiving his bride.

The king specially ordered construction of Andong Palace in the northern Anguk district for the wedding. The grounds also hold an older royal connection: King Sejong died here while staying at the residence of his youngest son, Prince Yeongeung. A new palace has risen on land with a long royal history.

Crowds lined the procession route to see the decorated palanquins and ceremonial attendants. Some expressed concern over the substantial cost to the treasury. For the moment, however, the royal celebration has brought unusual excitement to the capital.

Debate over reform and resentment among the old army units continue to unsettle the government. Soldiers whose pay has gone unpaid for months are said to be increasingly angry. People are watching cautiously to see whether the royal occasion can calm the country's troubled mood.
