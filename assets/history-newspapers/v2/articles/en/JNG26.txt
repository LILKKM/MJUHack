HANSEONG, May 1592 — Japanese Vanguard Enters the Capital's East Gate

Only 20 days after landing at Busan, the Japanese vanguard has entered the capital. Troops led by Konishi Yukinaga advanced through Heunginjimun, the east gate, followed by Kato Kiyomasa's forces entering from the Namdaemun side. The city has effectively fallen without a substantial battle for its defense.

The king had already departed northward several days earlier at a rainy dawn. Ministers and court attendants followed, while far too few troops remained to defend the city. News of General Sin Rip's crushing defeat at Tangeumdae in Chungju had thrown the capital into uncontrollable confusion.

Accounts say some angry residents set fire to palaces and government offices after the king left. Offices holding slave registers reportedly burned as well. Smoke rising around Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung and Changgyeonggung could be seen from a distance.

Japanese troops passing through the gates have established camps throughout the city. Those unable to flee are keeping out of sight. Rumors that the king will seek Ming assistance, and reports that the southern navy remains intact, offer the remaining hopes. Under enemy banners above the east gate, the capital's ordeal has begun.
