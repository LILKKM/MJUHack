GYEONGSEONG, April 1926 — Sunjong, Korea's Last Emperor, Dies at Changdeokgung

Sunjong, the last emperor of the Korean Empire, died on the 25th at Daejojeon in Changdeokgung. He was 53. His long-standing illness is reported to have worsened. On hearing the news, people dressed in white gathered before Donhwamun and prostrated themselves in grief.

Sunjong succeeded Gojong after Japan forced his father's abdication in 1907. Only three years later, in 1910, the country was annexed. The final royal council met that August at Heungbokheon within Daejojeon in this same palace before the annexation treaty was processed. Sunjong subsequently lived at Changdeokgung under the diminished title of King Yi.

An elderly mourner outside Donhwamun wiped away tears, saying that a ruler deprived of his country had died in loneliness. Students and young people kept vigil. Visitors from across the country are filling the streets of Gyeongseong.

The colonial government is sharply tightening security ahead of the June 10 funeral, mindful that Gojong's funeral seven years ago preceded the March First Movement. Police and military police are deployed throughout the city. Students and young people are nevertheless quietly discussing action on the funeral day. The emperor's death may again become a focus for national solidarity.
