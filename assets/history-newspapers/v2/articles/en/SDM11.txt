YEONHUI, GYEONGSEONG, 1920 — Yeonhui College Opens Its First Stone Building

Yeonhui College has completed its first stone building on the hills west of Gyeongseong. The three-story granite teaching block is named Stimson Hall after the American donor who financed it. Its pointed roof and sturdy stone walls stand prominently among the pines.

Missionaries including Underwood helped establish the college in 1915, when classes began at the Christian youth association building in Jongno. After receiving official approval in 1917, it secured extensive grounds in the Yeonhui fields. The school plans further buildings in the same style to form a larger campus. Work on Underwood Hall, intended as the main building, and Appenzeller Hall is expected to begin soon.

The college offers departments including literature, commerce, mathematics and physics, bringing modern education to Korean students. Applications have increased since last year's independence movement, school officials say. One student spoke of building learning as solid as the stone classrooms.

Questions remain over how freely Korean higher education can develop under the colonial government's education policies. Even so, the new building on the Sinchon fields has become a symbol of young Koreans' determination to learn.
