HANSEONG, October 1895 — Queen Killed as Japanese Raid Royal Palace

Japanese legation guards and Japanese irregulars stormed Gyeongbokgung before dawn on the 8th and killed the queen in an unprecedented assault. Witness accounts say they passed through Gwanghwamun in darkness and reached Geoncheonggung deep inside the palace. They found the queen among court women and attacked her with swords. Her body was reportedly carried to a wooded area and burned.

Hong Gye-hun, commander of the royal guard regiment, and royal household minister Yi Gyeong-jik were among those killed. An American military instructor and a Russian technician were in the palace. Reports that they witnessed the raid have helped bring the events to foreign attention.

There are widespread allegations that the newly appointed Japanese minister, Miura Goro, organized the attack to prevent the queen from working with Russia to drive out Japanese influence. Japan denies responsibility, presenting the violence as an internal Joseon dispute.

A pro-Japanese cabinet has taken office. An edict stripping the queen of her status has further inflamed public anger. Scholars in several regions are reportedly preparing righteous armies to seek revenge. The killing of the queen by foreigners inside the royal palace casts a dark shadow over Joseon's future.
