SEOUL, August 15, 1992 — Independence Park Opens on Former Prison Grounds

Seodaemun Independence Park opened today, the 47th anniversary of liberation, on the former Seoul Detention Center grounds in Hyeonjeo-dong. Established by the Japanese authorities as Gyeongseong Prison in 1908, the site served as a prison for nearly 80 years. After the detention center moved to Uiwang in 1987, Seoul began turning the vacated grounds into a park.

The park brings together Independence Gate, the site of Independence Hall and a memorial to patriotic martyrs. Some cell blocks, the execution building and watchtowers have been retained to preserve the memory of suffering here. The stone supports of the demolished Yeongeunmun remain in front of Independence Gate. The old tributary gate, the independence monument and the prison that held independence activists now form a single historical landscape.

Relatives of independence fighters and other citizens attended the opening. Walking beside the red-brick walls, they remembered those who died in the prison. One relative spoke of the profound feeling of being able to walk with fellow citizens through a place where their father had been confined.

The prison also held many democracy activists after liberation, some of whom were executed. Calls are growing for a place that remembers both independence and democracy. The city plans to continue restoring the old cell blocks as a history museum.
