HANSEONG, 1791 — Market Reform Ends Most Trading Monopolies

The king has accepted State Councillor Chae Je-gong's proposal to withdraw the licensed merchants' power to suppress unlicensed trade. Only the Six Licensed Shops, which handle silk, cotton, silk textiles, paper, ramie and fish, will retain that authority because they supply goods to the state.

The privilege allowed authorized city merchants to police sellers operating without licenses. Merchants used it to confiscate street vendors' goods or raise prices, leaving residents to pay more and threatening small traders' livelihoods.

Chae reportedly argued that letting small merchants trade without fear would lower prices and ease people's lives. Traders at Chilpae outside Namdaemun and Ihyeon near Dongdaemun have welcomed the decision. One Chilpae vendor expressed relief at no longer having to run from enforcement.

Licensed merchants in Jongno's Unjongga are protesting the loss of privileges held for centuries and are considering their response. The reform is expected to signal a major change in the capital's commercial life.
