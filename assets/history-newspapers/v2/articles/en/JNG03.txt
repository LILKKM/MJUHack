JAEDONG, HANSEONG, April 1885 — Western-Style Hospital Opens in Jaedong

Gwanghyewon, a state hospital offering Western medical treatment, has opened in Jaedong in the northern capital. It occupies the converted former home of Hong Yeong-sik, whose family was ruined on charges of treason after last winter's Gapsin Coup. Nearby stood the home of the late Pak Gyu-su, a pioneer of reformist thought who taught new learning to young aristocrats.

The hospital is led by the American physician Allen. During the coup he saved Min Yeong-ik, who had suffered life-threatening sword wounds, winning the king's confidence. Allen then proposed a national hospital, and the king agreed. Gwanghyewon means a house that extends kindness widely. The hospital is reportedly to be renamed Jejungwon, a house that helps the many, shortly after its opening.

Patients began lining up from the first day. Many fear Western methods involving cutting and stitching. Yet reports of a long-standing abscess cured in a day are drawing growing numbers seeking care.

Some see a poignant reversal in a failed reformer's house becoming a hospital for Western medicine. They are watching whether a reform movement marked by bloodshed can find a new purpose in saving lives.
