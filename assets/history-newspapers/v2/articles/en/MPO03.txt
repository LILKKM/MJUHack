HANSEONG, September 1866 — French Warships Reach the Capital's River Landings

Several foreign vessels have passed Ganghwa and steamed up the Han River to Yanghwajin and Sogang, close to the capital, causing alarm throughout the city. They are understood to be French warships sent in response to the execution of Catholic priests this spring.

The Mapo, Sogang and Yanghwa landings normally serve as Hanyang's larder, crowded with grain boats and cargoes of salt and salted shrimp. At the appearance of the smoke-belching vessels, boatmen fled and merchants shut their shops. Rice prices rose in the city, and some households began packing to leave. Crowds on the riverbank stared at the unfamiliar ships and cannon.

After spending several days sounding the river and surveying the terrain, the vessels withdrew. The court has reinforced troops along the river and renewed its resolve to repel the Western forces. The Daewongun is said to have ruled out reconciliation.

Rumors are widespread that the ships will return with troops. People at the landings fear for the capital's food supply if river traffic stops. The episode has brought home how directly the Han River landings expose the country to the outside world.
