GYEONGSEONG, May 1909 — Bethell Laid to Rest at Yanghwajin

Ernest Bethell, the British journalist who founded the Korea Daily News and sharply criticized Japanese encroachment, died on the 1st at the age of 37. He succumbed to heart disease at his Gyeongseong home. Large numbers of Koreans attended the funeral, and he was buried at Yanghwajin's foreign cemetery.

Bethell came to Korea to cover the Russo-Japanese War. In 1904 he established the newspaper with Yang Gi-tak and others. Its British publisher allowed it to evade Japanese censorship, exposing the injustice of the 1905 protectorate treaty. Its vigorous support for the National Debt Repayment Movement won a wide Korean readership. Japan twice pursued him through British consular courts. He received a custodial sentence and also served time in Shanghai.

Before his death he reportedly urged that, although he might die, the newspaper should live on to help the Korean people. Newspaper staff, students and merchants followed the funeral procession in tears.

Yanghwajin became a burial place for foreigners who died in Korea after the American physician Heron was interred there in 1890. At the death of a British man now resting on a hill overlooking the Han River, Koreans mourn the loss of another person who fought for their country.
