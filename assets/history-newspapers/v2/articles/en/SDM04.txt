HANSEONG, Summer 1897 — Old Envoys' Hall Reopens as Independence Hall

Mohwagwan, the hall outside Seodaemun once used to receive and entertain Chinese envoys, has taken a new name: Dongnipgwan, or Independence Hall. After founding its association last year, the Independence Club decided to renovate the aging building for use as its meeting hall. Repairs have now been completed and a new nameboard installed.

Since the early Joseon period, Mohwagwan had welcomed imperial envoys from Ming and Qing China. Kings came in person to receive them, making the hall a symbol of the old tributary relationship. The club described its new name as turning a house of service to another power into a house of independence. Directly in front, work continues on Independence Gate at the site of the demolished Yeongeunmun.

The club plans to hold public debates here on national affairs. Proposed subjects include expanding education, restoring public finances and preventing further concessions to foreign interests. News that anyone, whether aristocrat or commoner, may speak from the platform has attracted young students and merchants.

One member called it an unprecedented opportunity for ordinary people to gather and discuss the country's affairs. Others disapprove of public participation in government matters. Attention is turning to whether the old reception hall outside Seodaemun can become a forum for a new age.
